## 3. How we make decisions

Group decisions will be discussed through the WhatsApp group and during weekly meetings. Members are encouraged to share their opinions and reach a mutual agreement before making decisions.

If an agreement cannot be reached, the group will use a majority vote. If there is a tie, the coordinator will help the group reach a final decision.

Important decisions, task changes and agreed actions will be documented in the group's WhatsApp group.

Our group makes decisions through discussion during weekly meetings and our group chat. We try to reach an agreement that is suitable for everyone. If we cannot agree, we will vote and the majority decision will be followed. The coordinator will help resolve any disagreements. All important decisions will be recorded in our group chat so that every member can refer to them.

## 5. Rules for Git and for code

- Every member works under their own name: `git config user.name` and `user.email` are set on every machine.
- Every member makes at least 3 commits of real work. Nobody commits for somebody else.
- Commit messages say what changed and why. Small commits are better than one big commit.


- Each member must create and work on their own feature branch.
- Members must not push directly to the main branch.
- All completed work must be pushed to the shared GitHub repository through individual branches.
- Pull Requests must be reviewed by at least one other group member before being merged into main.
- Members should communicate before editing shared files to avoid merge conflicts.
- Every member is responsible for checking that their changes do not break existing functionality.

## 6. If someone does not contribute

If a member does not complete their assigned tasks or provide updates, the group will follow these steps:
- The coordinator will send a reminder after one week and ask about the member's progress.
- If the issue continues, the group will arrange a discussion to identify the problem and agree on a solution.
- If there is still no improvement, the group will inform the lecturer through email and provide relevant evidence of the situation.
The group understands that the lecturer may refer to Git commit history, individual contributions and individual Q&A when assessing and adjusting individual marks.
